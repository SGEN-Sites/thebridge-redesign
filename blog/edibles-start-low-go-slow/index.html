Cannabis 101

Edibles: Start Low, Go Slow

The hardest part of an edible isn't the dose. It's sitting on your couch at minute ninety, convinced it didn't work.

On this page

  1. Why eating it is different
  2. The clock
  3. The numbers
  4. What else moves the dial
  5. If you took too much
  6. Put it away like it matters
  7. Sources

Everybody knows this story. Someone eats a chew, feels nothing after an hour, decides it's a dud, eats another one and spends the rest of the night regretting the math. The fix is simple and hard at the same time: take less than you think, then wait longer than you want to.

Why eating it is different

Inhaled cannabis reaches the blood almost instantly. Swallowed cannabis takes the long way, through digestion to the liver. There, according to New York's regulator, THC transforms into a metabolite called 11-hydroxy-THC, which is several times more potent than THC when it is smoked. The state says that's why many people feel edibles more intensely than other forms.

The clock

The state's documents don't all give the same number, and you should know that. The Get the Facts flier says it may take up to two hours to feel the effect. The safer consumption guide gives a range of one to four hours. The adult-use information page says effects from edibles or beverages may not be fully felt until four or more hours after consumption. Plan for the long version.

And once it arrives, it stays. The state lists four to twelve hours for edibles, against one to three hours for inhaled products, which come on in about seven to ten minutes. Tinctures and lozenges, absorbed in the mouth, sit in between. The guide's minimum rule is to wait at least two hours before consuming more. Nothing bad ever came from deciding to wait until tomorrow.

The numbers

  • 10 milligrams: the most THC a serving of edible can hold in New York. The state says plainly that this may be too much for you.
  • 2.5 to 5 milligrams: what a low serving may look like for someone new to edibles.
  • Half or a quarter: you may have to cut an edible to get there.
  • The same or less: if you go again after waiting, the state says take the same amount or less, not a full serving.

Read the label for milligrams per serving and servings in the package before you open anything. The state's line on all this is worth remembering: you can always take more, but you cannot take less.

What else moves the dial

Body size, metabolism, product potency, how often you consume and what's in your stomach all change the experience. Our Facts page takes on the empty stomach myth directly, and its advice is to eat something first. Tolerance counts too: someone who has just started won't need what a daily consumer takes, so don't let anyone hand you their serving size.

Don't stack it with alcohol. The state says using the two together can make you more impaired than either alone. If you take prescription medication, ask your provider first.

If you took too much

It can be overwhelming and scary. It is also temporary, and that's where the state's advice starts. Drink water and rest. Breathe slowly. Put on something comforting and call someone you trust. Don't add other substances, and don't drive. If it's a medical emergency, call 911. For anything else, the Poison Center is at 1-800-222-1222.

Afterward, go back over the serving size and the setting, and write it down. That note is how next time goes better.

Put it away like it matters

Edibles can be confused with food, by adults, by young people and by pets. The state's edibles card says keep them locked, out of reach and out of sight, never near other food or drinks, and in the original child-resistant package. We go through the details in keeping cannabis safe at home. When you're ready to shop, edibles are here.
