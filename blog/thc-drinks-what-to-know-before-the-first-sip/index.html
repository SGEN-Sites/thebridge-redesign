THC Drinks: What to Know Before the First Sip

It looks like a seltzer and it counts like an edible. Read the can, mind the clock and pour less than you think.

On this page

  1. Count the servings in the can
  2. About the word fast
  3. Read the rest of the ingredients
  4. Two kinds of can
  5. At home and on the road
  6. Sources

A can in your hand feels familiar, and that's the trouble. You know how to drink a seltzer: open it, finish it, maybe have another. None of those habits work here. In New York's own breakdown, drinks sit alongside gummies and baked goods as food products, broken down through the liver as they're digested. Drink it like a bar drink and you'll find out why that matters.

Count the servings in the can

Don't assume one can is one serving. A 2025 public health paper in the journal Addiction points to drinks on the U.S. market with several servings per can, one example being 10 servings in 12 ounces, along with high-dose products such as 50 milligrams of THC in 2 ounces. New York edibles may hold up to 10 milligrams of THC per serving.

So read two numbers before you open it: milligrams per serving and servings per container. For someone new, the state says a low serving may be 2.5 to 5 milligrams, and it specifically suggests fewer sips rather than the entire serving at once. Pour a measured amount into a glass and put the rest away.

About the word fast

You'll see it on packaging. The same paper calls fast onset one of the major marketing claims in this category: most THC drinks, it says, are said to take effect within 15 minutes and last 60 to 180 minutes. There is some science behind the idea. Emulsified formulas can be absorbed more quickly.

But look at what got measured. In a 2026 trial with 20 people, a fast-acting edible reached peak blood levels at 30 minutes, about 30 minutes ahead of a standard one, with no significant difference in peak level or total exposure. A separate 2026 paper tracking 20 frequent users found the fast-acting product did not consistently produce earlier cognitive effects, and both products brought several hours of subjective impairment. Those studies tested edibles, not drinks. Even so, the lesson carries: quicker in the blood doesn't guarantee you can judge it in the moment.

The state's guidance doesn't carve out an exception. Its adult-use page says effects from edibles or beverages may not be fully felt until four or more hours after consumption, and its guide says wait at least two hours before taking more. If the can says fast, plan as if it doesn't.

Read the rest of the ingredients

Drinks often hold more than THC. The Addiction paper lists delta-8, THCV, CBD, CBN, melatonin and caffeine among the active compounds these drinks often contain, and says research has yet to extensively examine those combinations. Some products also borrow the look of cocktails. Don't take that as an invitation. New York says using cannabis and alcohol together can make you more impaired than either alone.

Two kinds of can

THC drinks have been sold well outside dispensaries because the 2018 federal definition of hemp measured delta-9 THC by weight, and liquid is heavy. That's closing. According to the Congressional Research Service, most of the new federal definition takes effect December 11, 2026, and it excludes finished hemp products with more than 0.4 milligrams of THC and similar cannabinoids per container. New York also runs its own license program for hemp products. The simple test for anything you drink: look for the universal symbol and a QR code to the lab results, and buy from a licensed store. The full hemp story is here.

At home and on the road

The state's storage card names drinks directly: infused products should not be stored in a shared fridge or pantry near other food. A can of infused seltzer next to the regular ones is an accident waiting to happen. Keep it in its original packaging, locked.

In a vehicle, keep it sealed. Neither drivers nor passengers can open cannabis packaging in a car. And a drink counts for driving the same as anything else. When you're ready, beverages are in the shop.
